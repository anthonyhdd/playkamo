How to camouflage something on tree bark

The one surface that comes with its own shadows built in.

Depth is the whole point

Most surfaces are flat, so the shadow a figure casts is the thing that gives it away. Bark is the exception: it is already full of deep vertical grooves with genuine shadow inside them. Place a kamo in a groove and its own shadow becomes indistinguishable from the ones around it.

Oak, plane and pine are ideal — deep fissures, strong tonal range. Birch and young beech are much harder: smooth, pale, and closer to a painted post than to bark.

Where to place it

Go vertical, and go where the trunk stops being regular.

  • Inside a fissure so the ridges frame the figure on both sides
  • At a branch junction, where the pattern breaks up naturally
  • On a patch of lichen or moss, which gives you a second colour to work with
  • Just below a knot or an old pruning scar

What gives it away

Painting horizontal marks on a vertical texture. Bark runs up and down; any stroke that runs across it is a line that exists nowhere else on the tree, and the eye finds it in under a second. Every stroke follows the trunk.
