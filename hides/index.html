Where to hide something in plain sight

The surface decides the round before you place anything. These are the ones people actually point at, and what each one does to a hide.

A hide fails for one of four reasons: the surface was too plain, the outline was unbroken, the brightness did not match, or a patch went unpainted. Which of those bites you depends almost entirely on what you pointed at — so pick the surface first.

  • Brick wall A repeating grid of mortar lines — the easiest outline to disappear into.
  • Gravel Maximum noise. Almost any paint job works — the danger is the shadow.
  • Grass Green everywhere, but the blades run in one direction and your paint should too.
  • Wood grain A desk or table: strong parallel lines that either swallow the figure or frame it.
  • Carpet & rugs A patterned rug is a cheat code. A plain one is nearly the hardest surface there is.
  • Tiles A hard grid with grout lines — great for outlines, punishing for colour.
  • Concrete Looks plain, is not: every stain, crack and pour line is a place to hide.
  • Tree bark Deep vertical ridges and real shadow. The best outdoor surface for a beginner.
  • Fallen leaves Chaos in three colours. Easy to blend, easy to overdo.
  • Sand Almost one colour, but ripples and footprints give you the lines you need.
  • Snow The brightest surface in the game — and the one where shadows are blue, not grey.
  • Bookshelf Vertical bands of colour and real shadow between the spines.
  • A cluttered desk Where most first rounds happen. Clutter helps, but only the right kind.
  • Kitchen counter Speckled worktops are ideal. Anything glossy is a trap.
  • Fabric & denim Folds create their own shadows — the best of them beat any paint job.
  • Asphalt & road Coarse aggregate, oil marks and paint lines. Better than it looks.
  • A plain white wall Hard mode. One tone, no lines, nowhere to hide — read this before you try.
  • Marble Veins are lines you did not have to draw. Follow them exactly.
