Where to hide something in plain sight

Where to hide something on a cluttered desk

Where nearly every first round happens, and where the results are wildly inconsistent.

Clutter is not automatically camouflage

A desk with three neat objects on a clear surface is worse than a plain wall: the eye scans four distinct shapes, and yours is the fourth. What helps is not the number of objects but whether they overlap and cast shadows onto each other. A tangle of cables, a stack of paper and an open notebook give you edges, depth and half a dozen colours in one square foot.

So before placing anything, ask whether the area you have chosen has real occlusion in it. If it does not, move ten centimetres to somewhere that does.

Where to place it

Look for places where something is already partly hidden.

  • Half behind a mug, a monitor foot or a laptop hinge
  • In the cable nest behind the keyboard
  • On the corner of a stack of paper, so an edge crosses the figure
  • In the shadow under a desk lamp arm

What gives it away

Desk lamps. A single strong light source throws one hard shadow in one direction, and every real object on the desk obeys it. A kamo painted without that shadow — or with a shadow pointing the wrong way — breaks the only physical rule the scene has. Check which way everything else's shadow falls, then match it.
