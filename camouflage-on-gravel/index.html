How to camouflage something on gravel

The most forgiving surface in the game, and the one place colour is not the problem.

Why gravel is easy

Gravel is dense random detail: hundreds of small edges, dozens of shades, no pattern for the eye to lock onto. Anything you paint that is roughly the right family of greys and browns becomes another stone. This is the surface to use when you want a high score without much patience.

It is also the surface where a fast, scruffy paint job beats a careful one. Smooth gradients look wrong here; blotches look right.

Where to place it

Use the size of the stones. A kamo among pea gravel reads as a big object; the same kamo among fist-sized stones reads as one of them.

  • Nestled between two larger stones so the outline is broken on both sides
  • In the darker band where gravel meets a kerb or a step
  • On a patch with mixed stone sizes rather than a graded, uniform area

What gives it away

Depth, not colour. Gravel has real three-dimensional shadows between the stones, and a kamo painted perfectly but sitting flat on top has no shadow of its own, which makes it read as a decal on a photograph. Place it low, in a dip, where the surrounding shadow does the work.

The other giveaway is scale: paint detail that is finer than the stones around it and the eye picks out the one area that is suspiciously smooth.
