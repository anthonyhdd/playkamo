Where to hide something in plain sight

How to camouflage something in grass

Everyone paints it green. The ones that disappear are painted with the grain.

Grass is a direction, not a colour

A lawn is not a green surface, it is thousands of thin lines all leaning the same way. Paint a smooth green kamo and it will sit on top of that texture like a bead. The hides that vanish carry streaks in the same direction as the blades — vertical where the grass stands, laid over where it has been walked on.

Colour is the easy part: grass is never one green. It runs from yellow at the base to blue-green at the tips, and dead blades run through it in tan. Copy three of those, not one.

Where to place it

Long grass is far easier than a mown lawn, because a mown lawn is a flat even field with nothing to break an outline.

  • At the edge of a border, where grass meets soil or paving
  • In a tuft that stands taller than the rest
  • Where a mowing stripe changes direction, so the eye is already crossing a line

What gives it away

In bright sun a kamo on a lawn casts a hard shadow across the blades, and no amount of green fixes a shadow. Shoot in the shade, or on an overcast day, and the same hide is twice as good. Overcast light is the grass player's friend.
